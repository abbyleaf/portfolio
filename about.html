§02 · Background

The short version.

I'm a systems-minded product designer with nine years spent on operational software. Dispatch consoles, internal CRMs, approval flows, admin panels: the tools a business actually runs on. I like problems that arrive tangled, with real constraints attached and no obvious first move.

Currently independent, working embedded with logistics and fintech teams. Most recently Field Ops Console, a dispatcher re-platform for a 1,200-driver fleet that cut median assignment time by 38%. Before that Atlas, where I owned activation and the internal admin surface, and Loom Ops, where a CRM consolidation across three sales orgs took forty-seven screens down to eleven.

My first design job was rebuilding a warehouse returns form nobody wanted to own. Eleven fields became four and a team of six stopped staying late on Thursdays. That was the whole lesson, and I've been chasing the same thing since: the leverage sits in the screens nobody photographs.

The way I work is unglamorous. I sit with the people doing the job — two weeks of shadowing beats twenty interviews, because nobody can describe the workaround they stopped noticing three years ago. Then I map the system before I draw a screen. Operational users are specialists, and the job is to design for their ten-thousandth session, not their first.

Off screen I make things with paper and glue: folded instruments, small interactive kits, activity sets for workshops with kids. Same instinct — a good paper tool and a good ops dashboard both hand someone something that fits their hand on the first try.
