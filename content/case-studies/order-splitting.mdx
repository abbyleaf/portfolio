<Section
  id="proposed-design"
  eyebrow="Proposed design"
  title="Planners can review both orders before confirming the split"
  intro="I placed the two resulting orders side by side so planners could see the suggested product quantities for one order and the remaining quantities in the other. I also kept the quantity fields editable for every product so planners could adjust the split before confirming."
>
  <ImageFigure
    label="Set a volume target, then review and adjust orders"
    src="/images/case-studies/order-splitting/locked/split-review.webp"
    alt="The SWAT Routes planning workspace, with the vehicles table above the orders table, where planners set a volume target and review the two resulting orders."
    width={3024}
    height={1784}
  />
  <Subhead title="The suggested split stayed within the target">
    <p>Calculating a split from a vehicle’s available capacity could produce fractional product quantities, which were impractical to load.</p>
  </Subhead>
  <ImageFigure
    label="Problem: A capacity target can produce fractional quantities"
    src="/images/case-studies/order-splitting/locked/fractional-problem.webp"
    alt="A target of 4 CBM entered for Split order 1. The first product row shows highlighted, unrounded quantities in both split orders, 4022.945744273065 and 3977.054255726935."
    width={3024}
    height={1010}
  />
  <p>The PM and I agreed that the system would suggest whole quantities within the capacity target. It would prioritise the heaviest products for a weight target or the bulkiest for a volume target, then add lighter or smaller products where they fit.</p>
  <ImageFigure
    label="Solution: Suggest whole quantities within the capacity target"
    src="/images/case-studies/order-splitting/locked/whole-quantities.webp"
    alt="The Split order modal over the planning workspace, with whole quantities suggested for each product and the total quantity and volume of each split order shown in the footer."
    width={3024}
    height={1784}
  />
  <Subhead title="Communicating that switching between weight and volume changes calculations and comparison tables">
    <p>The unit selector initially sat inside a separate preview panel, making it look like switching between weight and volume only affected the preview.</p>
  </Subhead>
  <ImageFigure
    label="Problem: Switching between CBM and kg seemed to affect only the preview"
    src="/images/case-studies/order-splitting/locked/unit-problem.webp"
    alt="Two explorations of the Split order modal. Exploration 01 puts the unit selector inside a separate preview panel. Exploration 02 puts it at the top right of the modal."
    width={3019}
    height={804}
  />
  <p>In reality, the selection also changed how the split was calculated and which units appeared in the comparison table. After exploring several layouts, I grouped the selector with the suggested split and comparison table to make both effects clearer.</p>
  <ImageFigure
    label="Solution: Group the unit selector, capacity target and table together"
    src="/images/case-studies/order-splitting/locked/unit-grouped.webp"
    alt="The Split order modal where the CBM and KG toggle, the capacity target and the comparison table sit together in one group."
    width={2960}
    height={2172}
  />
  <Subhead title="I designed the error around the empty order">
    <p>Reusing the existing validation pattern for the order split extension would make every product quantity field look mandatory. A product can go entirely into one order, leaving its quantity at zero in the other, as long as both orders contain at least one product.</p>
  </Subhead>
  <ImageFigure
    label="Existing validation: Missing values are flagged individually"
    src="/images/case-studies/order-splitting/locked/validation-existing.webp"
    alt="The existing Split order modal where each empty quantity field is outlined in red and labelled Value required."
    width={3024}
    height={2013}
  />
  <p>I designed the validation to check the total quantity in each resulting order and block the split if either order was empty.</p>
  <ImageFigure
    label="Solution: Show one error message and highlight the empty order’s quantity fields"

<Section
  id="reflection"
  eyebrow="Reflection"
  title="I learnt to ask how planners use the numbers they request"
  intro="When the planner asked to edit the volume on each product line, I realised I needed to understand what he was trying to decide. He already knew how much space was left in the vehicle and needed to work out which products and quantities would fit. That led me to use the vehicle’s remaining capacity as the starting point for the suggested split."
>
  <Subhead title="What I would track" small>
    <p><strong>Unused capacity.</strong> Compare the suggested and confirmed volumes with the capacity target to see how much space is left and whether planners’ edits close the gap.</p>
    <p><strong>Edits to suggestions.</strong> Track how often planners change the suggested quantities and observe why, such as to get a closer fit or keep products together.</p>
    <p><strong>Repeat splits.</strong> Track how often planners split a resulting order again and observe what prompts the second split.</p>
    <p><strong>Finding “split order”.</strong> In the prototype session, the participant needed prompting to find the row icon. I would observe more planners to see whether they find the action on their own and whether they use the row icon or the right-click menu.</p>
  </Subhead>
</Section>
