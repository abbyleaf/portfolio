<Section
  id="problem"
  eyebrow="The problem"
  title="Current order splitting did not help planners keep track of how products and their quantities were split"
>
  <Quote>
    <p>When an order exceeded a vehicle’s remaining capacity, planners had to split orders and decide which products and how many units of each to load onto each vehicle.</p>
    <p>However, SWAT Routes <Mark>split orders by weight or volume alone</Mark>, so planners had to <Mark>separately track the products and quantities</Mark> that went into each vehicle, and update those records whenever the order split changed.</p>
  </Quote>

<Section
  id="proposed-design"
  eyebrow="Proposed design"
  title="Planners can review both orders before confirming the split"
  intro="I placed the two resulting orders side by side so planners could see the suggested product quantities for one order and the remaining quantities in the other. I also kept the quantity fields editable for every product so planners could adjust the split before confirming."
>
  <VideoPanel
    tone="info"
    label="Set a volume target, then review and adjust orders"
    video="/videos/case-studies/order-splitting/locked/split-review.mp4"
    alt="A looping screen recording of the new split flow: the planner sets a volume target for Split order 1, previews the suggested split, and reviews and adjusts the two resulting orders side by side."
    size={{ width: 3720, height: 2160 }}
  />
  <Subhead title="The suggested split stayed within the target">
    <p>Calculating a split from a vehicle’s available capacity could produce fractional product quantities, which were impractical to load.</p>
  </Subhead>
  <ImageFigure
    tone="problem"
    framed
    flush
    label="Problem: A capacity target can produce fractional quantities"
    src="/images/case-studies/order-splitting/locked/fractional-problem.webp"
    alt="A target of 4 CBM entered for Split order 1. The first product row shows highlighted, unrounded quantities in both split orders, 4022.945744273065 and 3977.054255726935."
    width={2960}
    height={948}
  />
  <p>The PM and I agreed that the system would suggest whole quantities within the capacity target. It would prioritise the heaviest products for a weight target or the bulkiest for a volume target, then add lighter or smaller products where they fit.</p>
  <VideoPanel
    tone="solution"
    label="Solution: Suggest whole quantities within the capacity target"
    video="/videos/case-studies/order-splitting/locked/whole-quantities.mp4"
    alt="A looping screen recording of the Split order modal: the planner enters a target capacity in CBM, previews the split, and the suggested whole quantities appear for each product in the two split orders."
    size={{ width: 3720, height: 2160 }}
  />
  <Subhead title="Communicating that switching between weight and volume changes calculations and comparison tables">
    <p>The unit selector initially sat inside a separate preview panel, making it look like switching between weight and volume only affected the preview.</p>
  </Subhead>
  <ImageFigure
    tone="problem"
    label="Problem: Switching between CBM and kg seemed to affect only the preview"
    src="/images/case-studies/order-splitting/locked/unit-problem.webp"
    alt="Two explorations of the Split order modal. Exploration 01 has an Apply target to panel with a Cbm unit dropdown and a Preview distribution button above the table. Exploration 02 has a capacity target panel with a Preview button and a KG and CBM toggle for viewing conversions at its top right."
    width={3019}
    height={804}
  />
  <p>In reality, the selection also changed how the split was calculated and which units appeared in the comparison table. After exploring several layouts, I grouped the selector with the suggested split and comparison table to make both effects clearer.</p>
  <ImageFigure
    tone="solution"
    framed
    label="Solution: Group the unit selector, capacity target and table together"
    src="/images/case-studies/order-splitting/locked/unit-grouped.webp"
    alt="The Split order modal where the CBM and KG toggle, the capacity target and the comparison table sit together in one group."
    width={2960}
    height={2172}
  />
  <Subhead title="I designed the error around the empty order">
    <p>Reusing the existing validation pattern for the order split extension would make every product quantity field look mandatory. A product can go entirely into one order, leaving its quantity at zero in the other, as long as both orders contain at least one product.</p>
  </Subhead>
  <ImageFigure
    tone="problem"
    framed
    label="Existing validation: Missing values are flagged individually"
    src="/images/case-studies/order-splitting/locked/validation-existing.webp"
    alt="The existing Split order modal where each empty quantity field is outlined in red and labelled Value required."
    width={3024}
    height={2013}
  />
  <p>I designed the validation to check the total quantity in each resulting order and block the split if either order was empty.</p>

<Section
  id="outcome"
  eyebrow="Outcome"
  title="The client accepted the revised prototype"
  intro="After trying the revised prototype independently, the client identified both ways to split an order and said the flow worked for them."
>
  <Quote author="Client stakeholder">
    <p>We are good with the split order function here in the prototype. I noticed two options for splitting orders...</p>
  </Quote>
  <p>The revised prototype showed how planners could update product quantities within the split flow, reducing the need to maintain a separate record.</p>
  <p>I handed the design over to engineering before I left. I was unable to verify whether the feature was built or released, or whether it affected planning time, loading accuracy or vehicle utilisation.</p>
</Section>

<Section
  id="reflection"
  eyebrow="Reflection"
  title="I learnt to ask how planners use the numbers they request"
  intro="When the planner asked to edit the volume on each product line, I realised I needed to understand what he was trying to decide. He already knew how much space was left in the vehicle and needed to work out which products and quantities would fit. That led me to use the vehicle’s remaining capacity as the starting point for the suggested split."
>
  <Insights title="What I would track">
    <Insight icon="/images/case-studies/ui/speed.svg" title="Unused capacity">
      Compare the suggested and confirmed volumes with the capacity target to see how much space is left and whether planners’ edits close the gap.
    </Insight>
    <Insight icon="/images/case-studies/ui/table-edit.svg" title="Edits to suggestions">
      Track how often planners change the suggested quantities and observe why, such as to get a closer fit or keep products together.
    </Insight>
    <Insight icon="/images/case-studies/ui/flow.svg" title="Repeat splits">
      Track how often planners split a resulting order again and observe what prompts the second split.
    </Insight>
    <Insight icon="/images/case-studies/ui/screen-search.svg" title="Finding “split order”">
      Observe whether planners find <strong>Split order</strong> without prompting and whether they use the row icon or the right-click menu.
    </Insight>
  </Insights>
