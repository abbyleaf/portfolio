<Section
  id="solution"
  eyebrow="The solution"
  title="Let planners adjust the generated plan"
  intro="Manual Planning let planners adjust routes in response to disruptions and last-minute requests. They could assign orders to vehicles, move them between vehicles, remove them from routes or change the order of stops."
>

<Section
  id="decision-1"
  eyebrow="Design decision 01"
  title="Build on a familiar interaction to meet the deadline"
  intro="The previous logistics platform already used drag and drop for manual route changes. The team retained it to reduce design and implementation effort within a tight contractual deadline."

<Section
  id="route-rules"
  eyebrow="Route rules"
  title="Make route boundaries clear during resequencing"
  intro="Planners needed flexibility to adjust routes using their operational knowledge. The system blocked moves that made a delivery impossible, such as placing a delivery before its pickup. Changes that exceeded operational limits, such as vehicle capacity, could still go through. I worked with product and engineering to apply these rules in the planning interface and designed feedback that explained why a move was blocked."
>
  <ImageText
    title="Pickup before delivery"
    video="/videos/case-studies/manual-route-planning/locked/rule-1.mp4"
    alt="A looping screen recording of a pick-up stop being dragged below its related delivery in the route details table."
    width={2160}
    height={2160}
  >
    <p>A pickup could not move after its related delivery because the vehicle needed to collect the goods first.</p>
  </ImageText>
  <ImageText
    title="Delivery after Pickup"

    width={2160}

    <p>A delivery could not move before its related pickup because the goods would not yet be on the vehicle.</p>

  <ImageText

  <ImageText

<Section
  id="operational-exceptions"
  eyebrow="Operational exceptions"
  title="Allow changes while showing their consequences"
  intro="Changes that violated constraints such as vehicle capacity, delivery time windows or vehicle suitability remained possible. This let planners make exceptions, such as accommodating a customer’s request for an earlier or later delivery. I designed warnings to make the resulting violations visible and help planners investigate which orders, stops or routes were affected."

<Section
  id="reflection"
  eyebrow="Reflection"
  title="What I would do better"
  intro="We were working against a tight contractual deadline, with a large backlog of required features. Given more time and resources, I would prioritise two follow-ups:"
>
  <p><strong>Measure whether planners can complete their work in SWAT Routes</strong><br />I would track task completion, time taken, failed actions and repeated attempts, alongside research into which planning tasks still happen outside the platform and why.</p>
  <p><strong>Check whether violation feedback supports informed decisions</strong><br />I would measure feedback coverage and response time, then observe whether planners could explain which constraint was affected and where. Further testing would cover the complete journey from changing a route to deciding whether to resolve or accept a violation.</p>
