<Section
  id="route-rules"
  eyebrow="Route rules"
  title="Make route boundaries clear during resequencing"
  intro="Planners needed flexibility to adjust routes using their operational knowledge. Working with product and engineering, We distinguished between moves that made a delivery impossible, such as placing it before its pickup, and changes that exceeded operational limits, such as vehicle capacity. I then translated impossible stop sequences into blocked moves with explanation."
>
  <ImageText
    title="Pickup before delivery"
    src="/images/case-studies/manual-route-planning/locked/rule-1.webp"
    alt="Dragging a pick-up stop below its related delivery in the route details table."
    width={2160}
    height={2160}
  >
    <p>A pickup could not move after its related delivery because the vehicle needed to collect the goods first.</p>
  </ImageText>
  <ImageText
    title="Delivery after Pickup"
    src="/images/case-studies/manual-route-planning/locked/rule-2.webp"
    alt="Dragging a delivery stop above its related pick-up in the route details table."
    width={2160}
    height={2160}
  >
    <p>A delivery could not move before its related pickup because the goods would not yet be on the vehicle.</p>
  </ImageText>
  <ImageText
    title="Fixed start and end"
    src="/images/case-studies/manual-route-planning/locked/rule-3.webp"
    alt="Dragging a stop onto the fixed start or end of a route in the route details table."
    width={2160}
    height={2160}
  >
    <p>These locations defined where the vehicle began and finished its route, so they could not be moved during resequencing.</p>
  </ImageText>
  <ImageText
    title="Stops within route boundaries"
    src="/images/case-studies/manual-route-planning/locked/rule-4.webp"
    alt="Dragging a stop to a position before the route's start or after its end in the route details table."
    width={2160}
    height={2160}
  >
    <p>The vehicle’s start and end locations were defined by planning rules, so pickups and deliveries could not be placed before the start or after the end.</p>
  </ImageText>
</Section>

<Section

  eyebrow="Operational exceptions"
  title="Allow changes while showing their consequences"
  intro="Changes that violated constraints such as vehicle capacity, delivery time windows or vehicle suitability remained possible. This let planners make exceptions, such as accommodating a customer’s request for an earlier or later delivery. I designed warnings to make the resulting violations visible and help planners investigate which orders, stops or routes were affected."

<Section
  id="testing"
  eyebrow="Testing and refinement"
  title="Stop numbers alone did not explain the affected activity"
  intro="I tested the prototype with an experienced client planner, covering entry from both the Trips table and Route Details table."
>
  <Figure
    src="/images/case-studies/manual-route-planning/locked/usability-test.webp"

    width={780}

  />
  <p>The test supported grouping violations by type, but revealed that stop numbers needed more context. I added an Action column to clarify the affected activity and removed the Location Code column and placed location information into the relevant descriptions.</p>

<Section
  id="reflection"
  eyebrow="Reflection"
  title="What I would do better"
  intro="We were working against a tight contractual deadline, with a large backlog of required features. Given more time and resources, I would prioritise two follow-ups:"
>
  <p><strong>Measure whether planners can complete their work in SWAT Routes</strong><br />I would track task completion, time taken, failed actions and repeated attempts, alongside research into which planning tasks still happen outside the platform and why.</p>
  <p><strong>Check whether violation feedback supports informed decisions</strong><br />I would measure feedback coverage and response time, then observe whether planners could explain which constraint was affected and where. Further testing would cover the complete journey from changing a route to deciding whether to resolve or accept a violation.</p>
</Section>
