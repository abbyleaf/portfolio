<Section
  id="decision-1"
  eyebrow="Interaction design"
  title="Adapt drag-and-drop to the new planning workspace"
  intro={[
    "With a contractual deadline approaching, the product team decided to retain drag-and-drop from SWAT's previous planning tool rather than introduce an assignment modal. Reusing familiar interactions helped limit development effort while easing the transition for existing clients moving to SWAT Routes.",
    "I worked with product managers and engineers to define how these interactions would work across the three planning tables.",
  ]}
>
  <ImageFigure
    label="Order assignment flows and interaction logic"
    src="/images/case-studies/manual-route-planning/locked/assignment-flows-4.webp"

    width={6061}

  />
  <p>When planners drag an order from the Orders table into the Vehicles table, the system automatically determines its position within the route because no specific stop position was selected. Dropping it into the Route Details table lets planners choose where to insert the pick-up or delivery stop. Planners can also drag existing stops to reorder them within a route.</p>

<Section
  id="route-rules"
  eyebrow="Rules and exceptions"
  title="When to block a move or show a warning"
  intro="I designed how the interface responds to route edits based on the planning rules, blocking invalid moves while allowing others to proceed with a warning. This helped planners make changes without breaking essential route rules."
>
  <Subhead small title="Prevent invalid stop sequences">
    <p>Vehicles must collect goods before delivering them. The interface prevents planners from moving a delivery before its corresponding pick-up, or a pick-up after its delivery.</p>
  </Subhead>
  <Pair tight>
    <VideoPanel

      size={{ width: 3808, height: 2160 }}
    />
    <VideoPanel
      small
      compact
      label="Blocking delivery before pick-up"

      size={{ width: 3808, height: 2160 }}
    />
  </Pair>
  <Subhead small title="Keep stops within fixed route boundaries">
    <p>Each route has fixed start and end locations that cannot be moved. Planners can only reorder stops between these boundaries.</p>
  </Subhead>
  <Pair tight>
    <VideoPanel

  <Subhead small title="Warn when planning limits are exceeded">
    <p>Planners can still make edits that exceed planning limits, such as a vehicle’s load capacity. The interface displays violation warnings to identify which limits have been exceeded and where they occur in the route.</p>
  </Subhead>
  <VideoPanel
    small
    compact
    label="Allow edits but show corresponding warning"
    video="/videos/case-studies/manual-route-planning/locked/exception-warn.mp4"
    alt="A looping screen recording of a move that is allowed but breaks a constraint: the change is made and a warning appears beside the map."
    size={{ width: 3812, height: 2160 }}
  />
  <p>While designing these warnings, I raised an edge case with product managers:</p>
  <Quote>
    <p><Mark>What should happen when a vehicle has no capacity limit defined for an order’s unit of measurement?</Mark></p>
  </Quote>
  <p>The team decided to allow the assignment but adopted my recommendation to display a violation warning. I also proposed showing the assigned load even when the system could not calculate its capacity utilisation.</p>
</Section>

<Section
  id="testing"
  eyebrow="Testing and refinement"
  title="Stop numbers alone did not explain the affected activity"
  intro="In one session with an experienced client planner, I asked how they reviewed violation warnings in their previous tool and validated the prototype with them. They described spotting warnings in the Vehicles table, using the icons to identify the issue and opening the descriptions to understand what caused it. They used both type and severity to prioritise what to address."
>
  <Figure
    src="/images/case-studies/manual-route-planning/locked/interview-5.webp"
    alt="A video call recording of the Violation Usability Research Session. A slide on screen asks: What’s the first thing you usually do when a violation shows up? It shows the planner’s previous software, with the trips, action and order grids marked up in green."
    width={1680}
    height={929}
  />
  <p>The planner tried opening the prototype from both Vehicles and Route Details table. Grouping by type matched their expectations, but stop numbers alone did not tell them whether a violation warning pertained to a pickup, delivery or another activity.</p>
  <Figure
    src="/images/case-studies/manual-route-planning/locked/usability-test-6.webp"
    alt="A video call recording of the usability test, with the violations prototype shared on screen."
    width={1680}
    height={929}
  />
  <p>In response, I added an Action column, removed the Location Code column and moved location details into the descriptions where relevant. Version 2 remains a tested prototype.</p>

<Section
  id="reflection"
  eyebrow="Reflection"
  title="What I would do better"
  intro="We were working against a tight contractual deadline, with a large backlog of required features. Given more time and resources, I would prioritise two follow-ups:"
>
  <p><strong>Measure whether planners can complete their work in SWAT Routes</strong><br />I would track output variables like task completion, time taken, failed actions and repeated attempts, and also conduct research into which planning tasks still happen outside the platform and why.</p>
  <p><strong>Check whether violation feedback supports informed decisions</strong><br />I would measure feedback coverage and response time, then observe whether planners could explain which constraint was affected and where. Further testing would cover the complete journey from changing a route to deciding whether to resolve or ignore a violation.</p>
