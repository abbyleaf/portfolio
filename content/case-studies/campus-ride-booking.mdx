<Section
  id="how-i-got-there"
  eyebrow="How I got there"
  title="I started off by asking whether students needed to choose a travel service at all"
  intro="I explored a journey-first approach instead of the existing service-first flow. The idea was to let students find suitable rides without needing to understand the differences between services upfront."
>
  <Quote>
    <p><Mark>Tell us where you want to go, and we’ll show you suitable ways to get there.</Mark></p>
  </Quote>
  <Figure
    src="/images/case-studies/campus-ride-booking/journey-first-example.png"

    width={1680}
    height={1200}
  />

I used AI as a thought partner to prototype different journey-first booking concepts. Each explored how students could enter their pick-up and drop-off points before comparing available on-demand rides and fixed-route shuttles.

  <Figure
    src="/images/case-studies/campus-ride-booking/journey-first-ai.png"
    alt="Two overviews of journey-first booking flows explored with AI. The left, made in Figma Make, shows two rows of connected screens. The right, made with v0 by Vercel, shows two more rows of connected screens."
    width={1680}
    height={880}
    scroll
  />
  <Subhead title="Roadblock: Showing options across services required more engineering work than the timeline allowed">
    <p>The journey-first approach would require the app to show suitable on-demand rides and shuttle options based on where students wanted to travel. However, the developers advised that building this would take more time than we had before launch. I therefore focused on improving the existing booking flow, including adding a message to explain which service students should use to reach the dormitory.</p>
  </Subhead>
</Section>

<Section
  id="decision-2"
  eyebrow="Product decision 02"
  title="Showing departure times earlier undermined the accuracy of available travel options"
  intro="During the product review, the university requested that students see available shuttle departure times immediately after selecting their pick-up stop. This would help students decide between taking a shuttle or booking an on-demand ride based on departure times."
>
  <Figure
    src="/images/case-studies/campus-ride-booking/departures-requested.png"
    alt="The requested flow, in five screens: select a route, select a pick-up stop, select a departure time, select a drop-off stop, then review and book a ride."
    width={1680}
    height={672}
    scroll
  />

However, accurate information on the availability of shuttle seats requires both pick-up and drop-off points to be selected. A shuttle could appear available just based on the selected pick-up point, only to become unavailable once the drop-off is confirmed.

  <Figure
    src="/images/case-studies/campus-ride-booking/departures-final.png"
    alt="The final flow, in five screens: select a route, select a pick-up stop, select a drop-off stop, select a pick-up and drop-off time, then review and book a ride."
    width={1680}
    height={672}
    scroll
  />

**Solution:** We retained the booking flow where students selected their drop-off stop before viewing pick-up and drop-off times. This allowed them to see available departures and arrival times for their chosen journey.

</Section>

<Section
  id="reflection"
  eyebrow="Reflection"
  title="What I learnt and what I’d do next"
>

**Check feasibility earlier**\
The journey-first concept required more development than the timeline allowed. Next time, I would assess feasibility earlier so we can narrow the direction sooner.

**Measure whether the flow works**\
I would track booking completion, service switching, drop-off and availability failures to see whether students could find and book the right service.

</Section>
